Examples

What Pulse Motion makes from a single sentence. For each video: the request typed into Claude Code, the timeline you approve before any animation, then the resulting video. Plus two edits shown before and after, and three ways to use it day to day.

From sentence to video

Each example follows the three stages of a real session:

  1. The request: one sentence, exactly as it was typed. Pulse Motion then asks a few questions; the examples took the recommended answers.
  2. The approved timeline: the scenes, what each one does, its texts and its length. It is the only approval asked for on the quick path.
  3. The video: published as Pulse Motion made it, with no voice, with the music composed for it.

To start from one of them, the Ideas page gives sentences to copy for each kind of video.

Vibe Connection

A story, a mock silent retro film · 1 min 40 s, square (1:1).

1. The requests

A 1 min 40 film, made with Pulse Motion from a script written in advance: one first request for the first draft, then around thirty requests to redo each sequence, create the characters' gestures, add the effects and sound effects, and export it. They are all in the tutorial.

The first request, in full 
/pulse-motion:creer le film « Vibe Connection » décrit dans scenario.md (dans ce dossier) : lis-le en entier avant tout. C'est un faux film muet et rétro de 1 min 40 qui fait la promotion de pulse-motion, en 7 scènes composées librement, une par séquence, avec exactement les durées du scénario (20, 6, 17, 8, 23, 10 et 16 secondes) et ces noms de scènes : generique, installation, probleme, tournant, demo, preuve, pancartes. Genre storytelling, format carré 1:1 à 25 images par seconde, style tech-sombre, ton sombre, sans voix ni sous-titres, une musique composée d'ambiance inspirante (nappes et mélodie douces, sans sonorités chiptune) à 120 battements par minute, volume 0,45. Les textes à l'écran sont les cartons du scénario, mot pour mot ; aucun texte plus petit que 38 px. Pour ce premier jet : décors, cartons, caméra et objets de chaque séquence, avec les Tousfous du plugin (le dessin « touffu », hauteur 14 % de l'image en plan large) et seulement les poses et mouvements de la bibliothèque ; les gestes propres au film, les effets de particules et les bruitages viendront ensuite.

“the film “Vibe Connection” described in scenario.md (in this folder): read it in full before anything else. It is a mock silent, retro film of 1 min 40 promoting pulse-motion, in 7 freely composed scenes, one per sequence, with exactly the durations in the script (20, 6, 17, 8, 23, 10 and 16 seconds) and these scene names: generique, installation, probleme, tournant, demo, preuve, pancartes. Storytelling genre, square 1:1 format at 25 frames per second, tech-sombre style, dark tone, no voice and no subtitles, inspiring ambient composed music (soft pads and melody, no chiptune sounds) at 120 beats per minute, volume 0.45. The on-screen texts are the script's title cards, word for word; no text smaller than 38 px. For this first draft: the sets, title cards, camera and objects of each sequence, with the plugin's Tousfous (the “touffu” drawing, height 14% of the frame in a wide shot) and only the poses and moves from the library; the film's own gestures, the particle effects and the sound effects will come later.”

3. The video

Before and after: an edit in one sentence

A video is edited with one sentence, using /pulse-motion:retoucher. Here are two edits on “The cold chain”: on the left, the scene before; on the right, the same scene after. Both are rendered by Pulse Motion, from the edit applied; nothing is drawn separately. The previous version stays in the history: « annule » (“undo”) brings it back.

“Make the title punchier”

/pulse-motion:retoucher rends le titre plus percutant

“make the title punchier”

The title of scene 1 now comes in with a pop-in (apparition rebondie, #001 in the dictionary): it grows from nothing, overshoots its size slightly, then settles, instead of rising gently as the style would have it. Nothing else changes.

“Slow down scene 3”

/pulse-motion:retoucher ralentis la scène 3

“slow down scene 3”

Pulse Motion sets the pace of scene 3 in the timeline (0.8): all its timings stretch, and it goes from 13 s to 16.25 s. The texts and the drawing do not change.

Three ways to use it

Scenarios, not testimonials. These three people are imagined to show the journey, based on the three audiences Pulse Motion is made for. The times are rough estimates, taken from the tutorials in this wiki; the build, during which you have nothing to do, is counted separately.

Claire, food hygiene trainer

What she does. Claire is preparing a session on the cold chain for her trainees. She wants a one-minute video to show at the start of the session, then to upload to her training platform.

  1. She types her request in one sentence, answers the questions with the recommended answers, and approves the six-scene timeline.
  2. She adds her colours and her logo, then records her voice in the preview; the subtitles line up with her words.
  3. The day before the session, she finds the links scene too fast: « ralentis la scène 3 » (“slow down scene 3”).
  4. She exports the video to show it in the room.

The commands. /pulse-motion:creer, /pulse-motion:voix, /pulse-motion:retoucher, /pulse-motion:exporter (« présentation » (presentation) use).

Time spent. Half an hour for the first video, as in the first tutorial; one more hour for her brand kit, her voice and the high-fidelity export, as in the full tutorial (in French). Two minutes for the edit the day before.

Malik, solo creator on social media

What he does. Every week, Malik posts a cooking tip on Instagram and TikTok. He wants a 30-second vertical video that stops the scroll, without spending his whole evening on it.

  1. He copies a sentence from the Ideas page and changes the topic: Pulse Motion recognises a social media post, in vertical format.
  2. He doesn’t like the way the title comes in: he opens the moves page, picks one by watching it, and asks for it by name (« un pop-in sur le titre » (“a pop-in on the title”)).
  3. He exports for social media: the video keeps its texts clear of the apps’ buttons.

The commands. /pulse-motion:creer, /pulse-motion:retoucher, /pulse-motion:exporter (« réseaux » (social media) use).

Time spent. Five minutes for the request and the questions, as in the Quick start, a few minutes of editing, then the export. The following week, he starts again from the same sentence.

Atelier Nord, a small agency

What it does. The agency produces presentation videos for its clients, each in the client’s own colours. It wants two videos for two clients never to look alike.

  1. For each client, it applies their logo, colours and font, then saves this brand kit under the client’s name.
  2. For a client who wants a look of their own, it creates a personal style in one sentence (« un style papercraft aux couleurs pop-art » (“a papercraft style in pop-art colours”)), checked before use.
  3. For each new video, it applies the client’s saved brand kit, then approves the timeline with them.
  4. It exports the MP4 for the client’s website, and emails them a lightweight version for feedback.

The commands. /pulse-motion:creer, /pulse-motion:style, /pulse-motion:retoucher (« enregistre ma charte sous le nom dupont » (“save my brand kit under the name dupont”), « applique la charte dupont » (“apply the dupont brand kit”)), /pulse-motion:exporter (« web » and « courriel » (email) uses).

Time spent. About ten minutes per client for their brand kit, just once, as in the full tutorial (in French). After that, about half an hour per video, timeline and edits included.
