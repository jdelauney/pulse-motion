What ships with it

Catalogue

The styles, scenes, effects, characters, maps and formats that come with Pulse Motion. You don’t need to know them by heart: Pulse Motion suggests the ones that suit your video. This page helps you know what to ask for. Entrances, transitions and camera moves have their own gallery: the moves.

The twenty styles

A style is the look of the video: its shapes, its fonts, the way it moves. Your brand kit (logo, colours, fonts) is applied on top. To switch style along the way: « passe en style éditorial » (“switch to the editorial style”). To create your own: /pulse-motion:style.

  • welcoming · soft · humanchaleureuxWarm tones (terracotta, sage, peach), round shapes, pebbles that breathe. For a public service, a charity, healthcare. Fredoka and Nunito Sans fonts. Light tone.
  • elegant · demanding · calmeditorialTypography carries the video, like a magazine: fine rules that draw themselves, very large titles, slow movements. For storytelling. Fraunces and Instrument Sans fonts. Light (cream) or dark tone.

The styles in motion

Each style plays the same little story, “the cold chain”: two still frames (A, the title; B, the key figure), then its nine ready-made scenes in motion. The styles are grouped by family; open one to see it move, with its personality, its uses, what it is not, and where it comes from. On your computer, « montre-moi les autres styles » (“show me the other styles”) opens the same story, played live, so you can choose by watching.

The essentials

The chaleureux style · welcoming, gentle, human

For: Welcoming newcomers · Talking about health or prevention · Presenting a charity or association

What it is not: Not a childish or cartoon style: soft shapes, with no character in the spotlight. No sharp corners, no garish colours.

Signature: Organic shapes that breathe, cards with generous rounded corners, gentle motion that stops cleanly

Do: A direct, caring tone · Airy cards

Avoid: Sharp corners · Garish colours

Origin: an original Pulse Motion style

The clair-pedagogique style · composed, legible, reassuring

For: Training a team · Explaining a procedure · Presenting a product

What it is not: Not a terminal or magazine style: no grid, no monospace, no serifs. No pebbles or soft cards like chaleureux.

Signature: Large colour circles drifting in the background, text that slides in

Do: A single message per scene · Leave at least 1.5 s of reading time after the last entrance

Avoid: More than 4 points in a list · Text beyond the maximum lengths

Origin: an original Pulse Motion style

The editorial style · elegant, demanding, calm

For: Telling a story, a testimonial · Carrying an institutional message · Training at an unhurried pace

What it is not: Not a plain slideshow: typography leads, very large. No flat colour, no bounce, nothing rushed.

Signature: Thin rules that draw themselves, very large serif headings, text rising from behind a mask

Do: Let it breathe: wide margins, few elements · A single bright colour per scene

Avoid: Bounces and fast motion · Solid blocks of flat colour

Origin: an original Pulse Motion style

The energique style · bold, fast, punchy

For: Announcing a launch · Posting on social media · Launching a promotion

What it is not: Not a composed style: it shouts. No long text, no subtle gradients, no slowness.

Signature: Solid flat colours, giant capital headings that drop and bounce, moving bands

Do: Very short headings · One idea per screen

Avoid: Long paragraphs · More than three colours

Origin: an original Pulse Motion style

The minimal style · crisp, confident, essential

For: Presenting a product · Showing results · Announcing a decision

What it is not: Not an empty or cold style: the grid and oversized typography carry everything. No busy backdrop, no gradient, no more than one accent.

Signature: Swiss grid, lots of space, oversized typography, a single accent, short crisp motion

Do: Very short headings · A single accent colour per scene

Avoid: Decoration · Several colours

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “swiss-motion” style (MIT)

The tech-sombre style · precise, modern, technical

For: Demonstrating software · Presenting data · Making a technical tutorial

What it is not: Not a science-fiction screen: no garish neon, no glow everywhere. Not a warm style like chaleureux: monospace, sharp corners, a single accent.

Signature: Sliding background grid, text that types itself out with a cursor, monospace figures, glowing accent

Do: Short, factual sentences · A single glowing accent per scene

Avoid: Bounces · Bright warm colours

Origin: an original Pulse Motion style

Hand-drawn

The carnet style · spontaneous, warm, curious

For: Telling an idea or an approach · Summing up a workshop · Explaining with sketches

What it is not: Not a whiteboard: paper, no floating markers. Not a wax-crayon drawing: ink, with no wax or wash. Not chaleureux: drawn lines, not rounded cards.

Signature: Felt-tip sketches on cream paper: lines that wobble slightly and never quite close, hand hatching, a single line circling what matters, notes in the margin

Do: Let the video draw itself, one stroke after another · A single line for what matters

Avoid: Making text meant to be read wobble · Roughness that makes 9:16 illegible

Origin: explainroo, “paper” style (MIT) · take-one, “sketchbook” style (MIT) · lemo-opuscar, “one-line” style (MIT)

The crayon style · tender, handcrafted, patient

For: Raising public awareness · Telling a simple story · Explaining to children or families

What it is not: Not a sketchbook: wax and wash, not ink or hatching. Not a board: a picture-book page, on grainy paper.

Signature: Picture-book page in wax crayon on grainy paper: the wax only catches on the paper's raised grain, a translucent wash passes beneath and reveals, the lines quiver gently

Do: Let the page draw itself, one wax stroke after another · A wash to reveal what matters, not everywhere

Avoid: Making text meant to be read wobble · More than three crayons in the same scene

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “crayon-book” style (MIT) · take-one, “crayon-storybook” style (MIT)

The tableau-blanc style · clear, composed, educational

For: Explaining a concept step by step · Training in the room or remotely · Drawing a process on the board

What it is not: Not a technical drawing: drawn by hand, not with a ruler. Not a crayon sketchbook: marker on a glossy board. Not kinetic typography: real marker, written in order.

Signature: Marker on a glossy whiteboard: text writes itself in order, the ink darkens where two strokes cross, a marker floats with its shadow then parks off-screen, magnets hold the points

Do: Write every text in marker (data-lettres="ecrit") · Keep each marker's meaning for the whole video

Avoid: A cut on the board (prefer a whip pan or a slide) · More than three markers, or the accent everywhere

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “whiteboard” style (MIT)

The tableau-noir style · calm, schoolroom, attentive

For: Giving a lesson · Revising a concept · Telling a method step by step

What it is not: Not a whiteboard: no gloss, no markers, no magnets, no ghosting. The camera stays face on, in front of the board.

Signature: Chalk on a matt green slate: text writes itself with pressure, the board's grain bites into the chalk, a little dust as each stroke lifts, window light gliding across

Do: Few words, large letters, written in chalk · Keep the board alive (smudging, window light)

Avoid: Accent chalk everywhere · More than one erasing per video

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “chalkboard” style (MIT) · explainroo, “chalk” style (MIT)

Diagrams and data

The iso-album style · calm, caring, precise

For: Explaining a safety instruction · Presenting a welcome procedure · Showing the right order to go through

What it is not: Not a gag cartoon: small faceless characters, seen from behind. Not iso-infographie: an inhabited place and an everyday rule, not a system to take apart.

Signature: An everyday place in true flat-colour isometric view, small characters seen from behind doing the thing in order, and the rule written on the ground, letter by letter

Do: Act out the rule in order, one step at a time · Place the text on the ground, near what it describes

Avoid: Faces, expressions, gags · Several rules in the same place

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “isometric-picture-book” style (MIT)

The iso-infographie style · clear, structured, ingenious

For: Explaining a system or a circuit · Showing a site, a warehouse, a factory · Showing the inside of a machine

What it is not: Not low-poly 3D: flat three-tone blocks, with no lighting or perspective. Not a flat diagram nor plan-technique: volumes, not lines.

Signature: An isometric diorama of flat three-tone blocks that builds itself, thin labels along the way, numbers in Isotype; things open up to show their insides

Do: One colour per type of object, the accent for the sole subject · Build the system block by block, in the order it works

Avoid: Cast shadows, reflections, perspective · Too many objects: when the camera pulls back, they become illegible

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “iso-infographic” style (MIT)

The pictogrammes style · clear, rhythmic, universal

For: Giving an instruction or a user guide · Presenting a public or internal service · Showing a quantity in silhouettes

What it is not: Not an isometric infographic: everything is flat, face on. Not interface icons: signs, a grid and beats.

Signature: Signage that comes alive on the beat: geometric pictograms placed one per beat, a pattern grid of circles, half-discs and cells whose rows slide one cell per beat, signage typography

Do: Place each thing on a beat · One pictogram per idea, never an illustration

Avoid: Detailed or multicoloured pictograms · A move that falls between two beats

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “pictogram-motion” style (MIT)

The plan-technique style · precise, methodical, technical

For: Explaining a machine or a part · Training for maintenance · Presenting an industrial process

What it is not: Not tech-sombre: no interface, no keyboard, no typed text. Not a whiteboard: ruled lines, no hand. Not an isometric infographic: lines, not colour blocks.

Signature: White line on Prussian blue, drawn in the draughtsman's order, fine grid, title block, arrowed dimensions, section hatching, a single part in the accent

Do: Draw in order: frame, outlines, details, dimensions · A single part in the accent per scene

Avoid: Bounces and lively entrances · Too many lines drawn at the same time

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “blueprint” style (MIT) · explainroo, “blueprint” style (MIT)

The recit-donnees style · rigorous, clear, narrative

For: Telling a story with figures · Comparing results · Tracking a trend

What it is not: Not a dashboard: one idea at a time. Not a presentation with charts: the chart is the story itself.

Signature: The chart is the film: cream dot-grid paper, axes that grow with the story, one value in the accent, handwritten notes pinned up, the tip of a red-and-blue pencil, an empty dotted box for what we don't know

Do: One idea per chart, real data with its source · Make every move an operation on the chart

Avoid: Extrapolating or inventing a value · An axis that doesn't start at zero without saying so

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “dataviz” style (MIT)

Paper and print

The papier-decoupe style · handcrafted, cheerful, layered

For: Presenting an offer or a service · Welcoming and introducing a team · Telling a project in stages

What it is not: Not a flat style: depth comes from the shadows between layers. Not traditional red-and-gold paper cutting: papers from the brand kit, set out like a mock-up.

Signature: Cut papers laid in layers, with slightly irregular edges: real cast shadows between the layers, a sheet that lifts, a flap that rises to reveal the layer beneath

Do: Place each text on a sheet, and let the shadow convey the depth · Only one sheet lifted at a time: the one that matters

Avoid: Hard black shadows · Stacking more than four layers

Origin: take-one, “paper-cutout” style (MIT) · lemo-opuscar, “papercut-red” style (MIT)

The riso style · vivid, independent, graphic

For: Announcing an event · Posting on social media · Giving a brand character

What it is not: Not a flat style: the inks overlap and blend. Not an energetic poster: little motion, inks that settle, no dropping headings.

Signature: Two or three translucent inks overprinted on warm paper: a halftone dot screen, ink grain, plates never quite in register; text in the darkest ink

Do: Add one ink at each step, never more than three · Keep the text in the darkest ink

Avoid: Text on solid accent ink · A plate misregistration that changes on every frame

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “risograph” style (MIT) · take-one, “riso-pop” style (MIT)

Screens and announcements

The info-sans-son style · direct, legible, factual

For: Posting news on social media · Making an internal announcement · Making a key figure speak

What it is not: Not energique: the captions snap into place, without dropping or bouncing. Not a TV news bulletin: no permanent ticker, no presenter.

Signature: Giant caption that snaps in two or three pieces, a single keyword on an accent block, rolling figures with their source, dark ruled sheet with a double red margin

Do: One idea per caption, readable with the sound off · A source under every figure

Avoid: More than one keyword per caption · A loss shown by colour alone

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “caption-news” style (MIT)

The lancement style · premium, clean, luminous

For: Launching a product or an offer · Presenting software · Announcing something new

What it is not: Not tech-sombre: no terminal, no command prompt. Not minimal: dark, with light and depth.

Signature: Near-black background with a fine grid, soft moving light, interface cards that snap into place, a reflection sweeping across the heading, a giant thin-line number, a single accent

Do: One action per beat, the interface in the spotlight · A single coloured element per scene

Avoid: Grey too dark to be read · Too many elements moving at once

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “dark-keynote” style (MIT) · take-one, “monochrome-launch, dark-premium” style (MIT)

Animated

The mode-d-emploi style · calm, precise, vintage

For: Getting-started guide for a product · Step-by-step procedure for a workstation · Online help and how-to tutorial

What it is not: Not a warm style with breathing shapes like chaleureux: period flat colours, limited animation. Not a gag cartoon: a calm narrator who shows the gesture.

Signature: 1950s educational film: printed flat colours a few pixels off their broken outline, limited frame-by-frame animation, a guide with a still body whose hands alone do the work, numbered steps

Do: One step per scene, its number first, then what the hand does · Colour a part in at the moment it is named

Avoid: Moving the guide's whole body while it explains · Smooth, bouncy motion: everything moves frame by frame

Origin: lemo-opuscar, “midcentury-toon” style (MIT)

The particle effects

An effect serves a purpose: highlighting a figure, celebrating a conclusion, setting a mood. Never for decoration. Pulse Motion decides by itself which scenes get one: at most two effects on screen at the same time, and one or two in a whole short training video.

34 built-in effects, each shown exactly as the engine draws it.

air

The scene's air: voluminous smoke rising from the bottom, curling and flowing around the declared obstacles; acts as wind for coupled effects (data-milieu).

assembler

Particles from all over the screen assemble into the target element (data-ancre), from left to right; it appears when they arrive (at the end of data-dur, or earlier with "arrivee").

banc

A two-tone school of fish swimming together around the anchor (linked swarms); back layer, for the sea, the team, the collective.

banniere

A banner made from the anchor (heading, logo, image): the element becomes a cloth held by its top edge, flapping in the wind, then breaks loose and falls onto the declared obstacles (data-obstacle); the real element is hidden meanwhile.

Glowing embers rising slowly as they swirl; derived from the sparks (same settings).

braises-portees

Embers carried by the scene's air (data-milieu): they follow the plume and swirl around the obstacles; derived from the embers.

brasier

briser

The target element (data-ancre) shatters into shards flying from the point of impact, spinning and falling; they bounce off the scene's declared obstacles.

confettis

A burst of confetti in the brand kit colours that flutters down; ideal for a conclusion.

corde

A rope hanging from the middle of the zone's top edge (the anchor, or the marker), dangling and swaying; its length is the width of the zone.

desintegrer

paillettes

Adjusting an effect with a sentence

You sayWhat changes
« plus d'étincelles » (“more sparkles”), « moins de confettis » (“less confetti”)The number of particles, doubled or halved.
« plus lent » (“slower”), « plus vif » (“livelier”)The speed.
« en bleu » (“in blue”), « aux couleurs de la marque » (“in the brand colours”)The colour: a specific shade, or those of your brand kit.
« fais-les partir autrement » (“make them go off differently”)Another random draw, same effect.
« derrière le texte » (“behind the text”)The effect moves behind the content.
« plus tard » (“later”), « plus longtemps » (“for longer”)When the effect happens and how long it lasts.
« enlève les confettis » (“remove the confetti”), « ajoute de la neige sur l'intro » (“add snow on the intro”)The effect is removed or added.

Tip: on a light background, snow and dust, white by default, become invisible. Pulse Motion switches them to your brand kit colour or to a blue-grey; if you can’t see them, ask « neige plus visible » (“make the snow more visible”).

No effect fits? Pulse Motion can create one for your video, preferably starting from an existing effect (« des étincelles bleues et plus lentes, pour un sujet sur le froid » (“blue, slower sparkles, for a topic about the cold”)).

Maps

Pulse Motion draws maps of countries and French regions, overseas regions included: your export countries, your branches, a route. Countries fill with colour or light up, cities get a dot with their name, a line links two cities. Names place themselves, without getting in each other’s way or crossing the lines; in vertical, the map fills the screen and the overseas regions move below France. To ask for one: « une carte de nos pays d'export » (“a map of our export countries”), « nos agences en France sur la carte » (“our branches in France on the map”) (/pulse-motion:retoucher).
