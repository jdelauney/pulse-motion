Understanding

Concepts

The ideas Pulse Motion is built on, explained in plain words. You don’t need to know them to make a video: they help you understand what Pulse Motion suggests, and why.

From idea to video file

A Pulse Motion video isn’t edited the way it would be in editing software. It is described, then drawn by the browser, frame by frame, like a web page that moves. That’s what makes it editable with one sentence: changing a word in the timeline is enough to change the video.

Genre and style: two separate choices

Pulse Motion separates two things that often get mixed up:

  • the genre is what the video does: train, present a product, tell a story, post on social media;
  • the style is what it looks like: its shapes, its fonts, the way it moves, like a presentation theme.

Any genre can be combined with any style. The genre is worked out from your topic; it decides the structure of the video and the format, and ranks the styles from most to least suitable.

GenreSuggested structureFormatStyles suggested first
TrainingTitle → why (a figure or a warning) → steps or list → do’s and don’ts → conclusion16:9clair-pedagogique, chaleureux
Product presentationProblem → product → 3 benefits → key figure → call to action16:9minimal, tech-sombre
StorytellingSituation → difficulty → turning point → resolution → moral16:9editorial, chaleureux
Social media postHook in 2 s → 2 or 3 short scenes → conclusion9:16 (4:5 for the feed)energique, minimal

The third suggested style is deliberately contrasting: it shows you another possible direction. All twenty styles are presented in the catalogue.

The brand kit and the brand vocabulary

The brand kit is your brand’s colours, fonts and logo. It applies on top of the style: the style gives the look, the brand kit gives your identity. That’s what avoids the “all videos look the same” effect.

  • Two colours: the primary (titles, main elements) and the accent (highlights). Pulse Motion takes them from your logo, or from your words: « bleu marine » (“navy blue”).
  • Colours that are always readable: for each colour, Pulse Motion works out a version just dark or light enough to read against the background, and whether white or black should sit on a solid block of that colour.
  • Two fonts: one for titles, one for body text, embedded in the video.
  • A tone: light or dark, when the style offers both.

A brand kit is created during a video, then saved under a name (“atelier-nord”) so it can be offered for your next videos. If you change it later, /pulse-motion:reprendre tells you when a video uses an older version, and offers to update it.

The brand vocabulary goes with the brand kit: these are the words of your trade (acronyms, product names), with their exact spelling and the forms to avoid. The check flags any mistake in these words. Not to be confused with the personal glossary, which keeps track of the tool’s words that have already been explained to you.

The timeline

The timeline is the list of scenes in order, like a running order for a TV show. It’s the heart of the video: a single file, deroule.json, holding everything that one sentence can change.

For the whole videoFor each scene
The title, the genre, the pathThe text shown on screen
The format (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5) and the frames per secondThe spoken text, if the video has a voice
The style and the brand kit settings specific to this videoThe length, and the pace (slower, livelier)
The music, the voice, the subtitles, the sound effectsThe transition to the next scene

Why it matters: the text is never written in the scenes, only in the timeline. So « Change le titre » (“Change the title”) changes a single line, with no risk of breaking the animation.

The pacing rules

  • One idea per scene. If the text holds two ideas, that’s two scenes.
  • Time to read: a scene lasts at least 1.5 s + 0.3 s per word shown.
  • Nothing still for more than 2.5 s: a drifting background or a staggered entrance is enough.
  • End on a still frame lasting at least half a second.
  • Short text: a title of 40 characters at most, scene text of 80 at most, numbers written as figures.

Ready-made scenes and composed scenes

A scene is one animated screen of the video, like a slide that moves. Each style offers nine ready-made scenes, one per role:

SceneRoleSuggested length
titreOpen the video or a chapter7 s
liste2 to 4 points revealed one by one12 s
chiffreA key figure that counts up to its value7 s
comparaisonTwo columns: before / after, do / don’t12 s
etapeA numbered step in a procedure8 s
alerteA point to watch out for, a mistake to avoid8 s
schemaThree steps linked by arrows8 s
moyeuOne idea in the centre, 3 to 5 parts around it, linked by arrows10 s
conclusionThe key message and the logo6 s

Lengths vary a little from one style to another (the energetic style is shorter). When none of them fits, the animator composes the scene freely: a character (in French), a background, a moving camera.

The difference matters when you change style: a ready-made scene is carried over as it is into the new style; a composed scene has to be rewritten.

The animators

An animator is a specialised Pulse Motion assistant that writes one scene. During the build there is one per scene, and they all work at the same time: a 10-scene video doesn’t take ten times longer than a one-scene video. Pulse Motion itself decides on particle effects and hands them to the animators concerned, to keep the video understated.

The characters

A character is an animated actor in the scene: it walks in, points to a figure, waves, changes expression, and can even speak the scene’s text with your voice. They all share the same skeleton, dressed as a stick figure, as a drawing made on request or as your own illustration; so they all strike the same poses and make the same moves. Everything is explained on the characters page (in French).

The path: quick or guided

At the start, you choose your path, that is, how many times Pulse Motion stops to ask for your approval.

PathYour approvalsWho it’s for
Quick (default)Just one: the timeline, with the choice of style in the same questionYou want to see the video fast, and edit it afterwards.
GuidedThree: the timeline, then the style, then the final videoYou want to approve each step, or the video is important.

Either way, nothing is animated before you approve the timeline, and everything can still be edited afterwards.

The preview

The preview is the video itself, open in your browser, with a playback bar. It’s served by a small server on your computer (an address starting with http://localhost:…): nothing goes out to the internet.

  • It reloads on its own after every edit, at the same point.
  • You can drive it from the keyboard: Space play/pause, ← → frame by frame, Shift + arrow to change scene, B to loop a scene.
  • It hosts the voice studio (the Voix (Voice) button) and an MP4 button that exports directly in the browser.

The video is deterministic: a given moment always shows exactly the same frame. That’s what makes it possible to check it frame by frame, and to export it without stutter, however powerful the computer is.

The automatic checks

After every build and every edit, Pulse Motion checks the video in two stages: first the timeline and the text, then the rendered video, in Chrome or Edge without a window, frame by frame.

The safe cases are fixed on their own, and Pulse Motion tells you in one sentence what it did. The rest is fixed by Pulse Motion itself, in two rounds at most; whatever resists is reported to you.

What is checkedFixed on its own?
A scene too short to read its textYes: lengthened, by 50% at most
Dead time at the end of a scene (nothing moves for more than 2.5 s)Yes: scene shortened, without going below the reading time
A video ending without a still frameYes: last scene lengthened by one second at most
French typography (spaces before ; : ! ?, « » quotation marks, ’ apostrophe, ellipses)Yes, in the timeline text
A capital letter missing its accent (« Etape » → « Étape »)Yes
Text spilling out of its box or off the frameYes: size reduced down to 80%, never below readable
Two overlapping elements, a hidden logo, text too close to the edgeNo: reported and fixed in the scene
In vertical format: text under the social media buttonsNo: reported
The real contrast of a text against what lies behind its lettersNo: reported
More than 3 flashes per second over a quarter of the frameNo: error, risk of an epileptic seizure
A word from the brand vocabulary misspelt, a word written twice in a rowNo: reported

Each automatic fix is backed up first: « annule » (“undo”) reverses it. If you asked for a specific length or spelling, Pulse Motion checks without fixing, to respect your request.

Tip: the frame check needs Chrome or Edge. Without them, Pulse Motion tells you the render hasn’t been checked: the video is made, but overflows haven’t been measured.

Tempo: timing the picture to the music

With composed music, a score, or a file whose tempo you know, the video takes on the music’s tempo, in beats per minute. At 120 beats per minute, there’s a beat every half-second. Animations can then start on the beat.

It’s the music that fits the picture, not the other way round: composed music (or a score timed to the scenes) starts again at the beginning of each scene. Scene lengths stay free, and the check can fix a length on its own without putting the music out of step.

Music from a file, on the other hand, doesn’t move: if you give its tempo, each scene must start on a beat (at 120 beats per minute, every half-second), and the check no longer fixes a length on its own, so as not to put the picture out of step with the music.

Transitions

The transition is the passage from one scene to the next. It lasts half a second.

TransitionFor
fonduThe default choice, everywhere.
glissementMoving forward through a sequence (steps).
balayageChanging section.
rideauAn opening from the centre (editorial style).
zoomThe scene comes closer (energetic style).
coupeA hard cut, with no overlap (energetic and minimal styles).
whipA fast, blurred whip pan: an energetic change.
pousseeThe new scene pushes the old one out of frame: a logical sequence, a slideshow.
traverseeYou go through the scene into the next one: diving into the subject.
cercleThe scene opens in a growing circle: a playful or retro tone.
voletsThe scene appears in strips: a graphic tone.
retournementThe scene flips like a card: a question, then its answer.
aucuneLinking two scenes that answer each other (before / after).

Two or three families at most in one video, and the same one for scenes of the same kind.

Transitions, like text entrances and camera moves, also have a trade name and a number in the move dictionary: « un whip pan entre la scène 2 et la 3 » (“a whip pan between scenes 2 and 3”) or « #042 » are understood. For the list: « quels mouvements existent ? » (“which moves are there?”).

The explainer video

An explainer video helps people understand a mechanism: a circuit, a process, a calculation. The « Vidéo explicative » (Explainer video) template builds it in six parts: a hook, the problem and its causes, the mechanism in three steps, a close-up on one detail, a figure, the summary. Example: « Du devis au paiement : le trajet d’une facture » (“From quote to payment: the journey of an invoice”), on the Video ideas page.

  • What is said is shown: an arrow draws itself from “Client” to “Server” when the voice says “sends”, a word is circled by hand as it’s spoken, a chart grows bar by bar over the years mentioned, a spreadsheet formula builds up step by step.
  • The camera finds its own shots: an opening shot, a move in when there’s time to look, a final pull-back over the whole idea, never cutting off any text. « Laisse la caméra bouger toute seule » (“Let the camera move on its own”), or « garde la caméra fixe scène 2 » (“keep the camera still in scene 2”).
  • Small, soft sounds go with each entrance (a card, an arrow, a list point, a bar counting up): « ajoute des petits sons » (“add little sounds”).
  • The overall pace is set with one sentence: « plus rythmé pour les réseaux » (“punchier for social media”), « plus posé » (“calmer”).

In an explainer video, the automatic camera and the small sounds are on by default; elsewhere, they stay off until you ask for them.

The voice can be yours, or a synthetic voice made on your computer, with no account or connection once installed (about 300 MB, with your approval). It’s a single French female voice, decent but less natural than a real voice. Unlike the computer’s own voice, it can be delivered as it is, and its credits are added automatically. See /pulse-motion:voix.

The two exports

The same video gives you two deliverables:

  • the animated page (sortie/<titre>.html), always up to date: a single file that contains everything (images, fonts, sound) and opens offline, ready to put on a website;
  • the MP4 file, made on request by /pulse-motion:exporter, ready to publish or send.

The MP4 can be made in two ways:

The edit history

Before every edit, Pulse Motion copies the state of the video (timeline, scenes, files, brand kit) into a hidden folder, .historique/. The last 20 copies are kept. « annule » (“undo”) restores the most recent one; said again, it goes back in time, copy after copy.

Privacy

When you record an app with /pulse-motion:filmer, what appears on screen may be personal: addresses, customer names, an IBAN. Pulse Motion follows four rules.

  • The blur is in the frames themselves. It’s applied during recording, before the frame is written to disk: no sharp frame is kept, and nobody can “remove” the blur from the delivered page. Passwords, email addresses, phone numbers, IBANs, card and national insurance numbers, and address and date-of-birth fields are blurred automatically. Anything else (a name, a face, an amount), Pulse Motion points out to you when it looks at the contact sheet, and you decide.
  • Your password never goes through Pulse Motion. You sign in yourself, in a visible window; Pulse Motion doesn’t see what you type, never types a password, and its browser’s password manager is turned off.
  • The internet is only used for recording, and only after you’ve approved that site, one per recording. If the page tries to take you elsewhere, navigation is blocked. After that, the video never needs it again: the preview and the export read the recording kept in the folder.
  • The clicks are real. Pulse Motion warns you before the first recording: an action taken in the app (save, send) isn’t undone afterwards. A demo account is the safest option.
What is keptWhere
The recording (frames already blurred, click positions) and your approval for the siteThe video’s folder, under films/. A take doesn’t go into the edit history: its blur is permanent.
Your login to a site (its cookies, as in any browser)Your personal Pulse Motion folder, separately for each site, never in the video’s folder or in an exported library. « Oublie ma connexion à … » (“Forget my login to …”) deletes it.
Your passwordNowhere.

The personal glossary

Pulse Motion talks to you without jargon. When it needs to explain one of the tool’s words (timeline, scene, brand kit, export…), it does so once, with an everyday image, and notes it in your personal glossary. Next time, it doesn’t explain it all over again. You can read and correct this glossary: it’s a text file, lexique.md, in your personal Pulse Motion folder. The glossary of this wiki (in French) lists these words.

Your library

What you keep for all your videos makes up your personal library: your saved brand kits, your personal styles, the effects and characters you’ve kept, and the preferences it remembers: « jamais de rebond » (“never any bounce”).

  • Keep. « Garde cet effet de neige pour mes prochaines vidéos » (“Keep this snow effect for my next videos”), « garde ce personnage » (“keep this character”): it joins your library, along with what it needs (a character’s poses and moves, an effect’s image). When you’ve just drawn a character or invented a move, Pulse Motion offers to keep it. A name already used by something that comes with Pulse Motion can’t be used: it suggests another one.
  • Find again. When Pulse Motion suggests an effect or a character, the ones in your library are in the list, alongside the ones that come with it.
  • By copy. An item from your library is copied into the video that uses it. The video stays complete: you can copy it or send it on its own. And changing your library never changes a video that’s already made.
  • Take it to another computer. « Emporte mes chartes sur mon autre ordinateur » (“Take my brand kits to my other computer”): your whole library, or whatever you choose, goes into a single ZIP file that your file explorer can open. You copy it yourself (USB stick, email); nothing goes over the internet. On the other computer: « importe ma bibliothèque » (“import my library”). Whatever is already there is never replaced without your approval.

What doesn’t go with it: your logins to the sites you’ve recorded, the list of your videos and the error log stay on the computer where they were made.

Where your files are

Pulse Motion works without the cloud: nothing is sent to a video service. Everything is on your computer, in two places.
