Step 1 · Create

/pulse-motion:filmer

Film a web app or a website for a tutorial, or start from a video or screenshots of your screen (« filme comment on crée une facture sur app.exemple.fr » (“film how to create an invoice on app.exemple.fr”), « j'ai enregistré mon écran » (“I’ve recorded my screen”), « floute les noms des clients » (“blur the customers’ names”)).

    Description

    This is the command for software tutorials: showing how to do something in an app (“we go to Invoices, click New invoice, type the customer, save”). You describe the journey in your own words; Pulse Motion does it itself in the app, step by step, and films it.

    1. You describe the journey, as you would to a colleague, and give the starting address. Pulse Motion reads it back to you as numbered sentences: you approve or correct it.
    2. For a live website, it asks for your consent, naming the site. If the app asks for a login, you sign in yourself, in a window that opens on your screen.
    3. It films: an image of each state of the app, the short reactions after each click, and the position of each click. A few seconds per step.
    4. It looks at the result with you: a contact sheet, one image per step. It points out anything that looks personal and offers to blur it.
    5. You choose the take you keep, or you film again.
    6. The footage goes into the video: one scene for one to three steps. The pointer, clicks, zooms and spotlight are added by Pulse Motion, after filming.

    It can also start from a video of your screen (an installed program, filmed with your computer’s own tool), an animation (a GIF) or screenshots: the same pointer, zooms and spotlight go on top.

    You can run it on its own, to add footage to a video that already exists. /pulse-motion:creer also calls it by itself for a tutorial subject.

    Why use it

    • Everything can be edited without filming again. The pointer, zooms, spotlight and the time between two clicks are added afterwards: « le pointeur va trop vite » (“the pointer is going too fast”), « zoome sur le bouton » (“zoom in on the button”) take one sentence.
    • The footage is kept with the video. Once filmed, the app is never visited again: neither the preview nor the export needs the internet, and two exports give exactly the same images.
    • Personal data is blurred in the images themselves, at filming time: email addresses, phone numbers, IBANs, card numbers. No sharp image is kept.
    • Your password stays yours. Pulse Motion never types it, never sees it and never writes it anywhere, even if you give it in the conversation.
    • A failed take can be redone. Each filming is a new take; you choose the one that goes into the video.

    What it produces

    • The footage, stored in the video’s folder, under films/: the images of each step, already blurred, and the position of each click. Each filming adds a take there.
    • A contact sheet per take: one image per step, with the click target and the number of blurred areas. Pulse Motion looks at it with you.
    • Filmed scenes in the video, one for one to three steps, shown in a browser frame (or a phone, tablet or window frame).

    What is kept on your computer, and what isn’t, is explained in the Privacy section of the concepts.

    Risks and precautions

    • The clicks are real. Pulse Motion really carries out the actions in the app: a saved invoice is saved, a sent message is sent, and nothing is undone afterwards. Use a demo account if you have one.
    • The automatic blur doesn’t recognise everything. It spots email addresses, phone numbers, IBANs, card and social security numbers, address and date-of-birth fields. Names, faces or amounts are pointed out by Pulse Motion when it looks at the contact sheet, and you decide.
    • A blur added afterwards is permanent in that take: to remove it, you have to film again. A video already exported before that blur keeps the sharp image: export it again.
    • One site per filming. Your consent applies to that site; if the page tries to go elsewhere, navigation is blocked and Pulse Motion tells you.
    • Don’t touch the window while filming, when it is visible (after signing in): a few seconds per step.

    Tip: start with the test app (« montre-moi sur l'appli d'essai » (“show me on the test app”)): you see the whole journey, signing in and blurring included, without touching a real app.

    Problems and solutions

    Pulse Motion can’t find the button or the field

    The take stops at that step. Pulse Motion tells you what it sees instead, and shows you the image. Describe the target differently, using the text actually displayed (« le bouton Créer » (“the Create button”)), its position (« le deuxième Modifier » (“the second Edit”)) or where it is (« dans la fenêtre » (“in the window”)), then film again. Actions already carried out are not undone.

    The page asks me to sign in again

    The sign-in has expired, or the site asks for a login that Pulse Motion doesn’t have. Reply « je dois d'abord me connecter » (“I need to sign in first”): the window opens, you sign in, then you say « c'est fait » (“done”).

    You can’t see the page loading

    By default, Pulse Motion moves from one page to the next with a fade. Say « on ne voit pas le chargement » (“you can’t see it loading”): the next take keeps the loading images.

    My screen recording is refused

    Its format can’t be read by your computer’s browser. Install ffmpeg with /pulse-motion:outils, then give the file again.

    My screen recording is longer than 3 minutes

    Pulse Motion asks you which part to keep: the start and end, in minutes and seconds (« de 0:12 à 1:05 » (“from 0:12 to 1:05”)).

    Pulse Motion warns about a heavy video

    Above 25 MB of images in the page, it tells you before the export. Delete the takes you don’t need, shorten the reactions, or show fewer steps per scene.

    « Chrome ou Edge est introuvable » (“Chrome or Edge can’t be found”)

    Filming goes through Chrome or Edge. Install one of the two, then check with /pulse-motion:outils. See the requirements.
